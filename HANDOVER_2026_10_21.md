## 7. The outcome contract (the framework's decision surface)

`src/progressdesk/outcomes.py`: a closed vocabulary of 16 conditions, one chain, and a versioned
word table.

```
cancelled -> terminal
answered  -> the work is the builder's report and the gate's business
not answered -> capacity (never nudged) | provider fault (interrupted, nudged)
              | configuration (a human is needed) | harness fault | unknown
unknown    -> interrupted (the recoverable default) AND recorded as
               outcome.unknown-condition AND handed to the observer
```

Words may only **narrow an unknown** into a known condition; they never change a known condition
and never decide an action. The Pi adapter keeps the harness's facts (`stopReason` enum, provider,
model, response id, the provider's error code from the diagnostics); the OpenCode adapter keeps a
structured provider error when given one. **MS4** delivers this into the product; its instrument
(`tools/final-delivery/ms4_gate.py`) already self-tests as *passed* against the plant source.

## 10. Traps, paid for once

- **A feature must fit one 900 s attempt.** `ms2-mechanical-propagation` spent 115,599 input and
  59,577 output tokens over the budget and delivered nothing. It was split by the owner into
  `ms2-fix-apply` and `ms2-fix-supersession`; both then delivered. **Size features to ~10 minutes
  of builder work.**
- **A wall-clock expiry is an infrastructure fault, not a non-delivery**, so it never enters
  JACK's split ladder. The plant's own split path still has **no live evidence** — it is named in
  the final report as outstanding. Exercising it deliberately is a legitimate small task.
- **The free provider errors.** It ends generations with `finish_reason: error`; before the
  contract that killed rounds, now it nudges.
- **The observer was the most expensive thing in the build** (11 judgements, ~33 of 65 minutes,
  60–120k input tokens each). It now gets the finding and the involved brief only.
- **The journal was drowning**: 258 sweep events for 45 distinct facts, half of it a brief lint.
  A fact is now recorded when it changes.
- **The instrument must distinguish a refusal from a defect.** `gatekit.Probe.refusal()` treats a
  correct refusal as a pass; `available()` treats a missing operation as a defect; instrument
  faults are reported separately as `unavailable`, never as a product verdict.
- **`project.create` needs the chain and the draft digest** in the new product (F-CR1): a gate that
  creates a project must pass `definition_refs` and the `chain_digest`, and must sign the VISP and
  FUSP first or the refusal is correct behaviour.
