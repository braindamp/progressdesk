A team is building a software product with an AI "builder workshop," and I need you to design
the information channel that feeds it. I want a design, not a survey — commit to one approach
and defend it.

## The setup

Work is split into **milestones**, and each milestone into **feature slices**. One slice is one
builder agent's job. Slices are produced by a **Ralph loop**: the same agent wakes, reads, edits,
verifies, and repeats, many times, until it declares the slice done. Iteration count is high and
unpredictable, which is the entire problem I am asking you to solve.

Three documents are canonical and pre-existing:

- a **vision document** (why this product exists, strategic intent)
- a **functional specification** (what the product must do, behaviourally)
- a **technical specification** (architecture, interfaces, conventions, constraints)

They were written once, by humans, for humans. They are long. They are not going to be rewritten
per slice.

## What a builder actually is, technically

- A subprocess with a **single-use token**, a **tools allowlist** (typically a shell, file read,
  file write, and nothing else), and a hard **timeout**.
- It has **read access to the repository** and can therefore read any file in it, including the
  three documents, if it chooses to.
- It has **no network** and **no memory between iterations** except what it writes to disk in its
  own workspace.
- It is running inside a **job object** that will kill it without warning.
- It is **not permitted to write to the central event log**; it leaves requests in an inbox that
  the control host drains. This matters because I want to know exactly what a builder can and
  cannot be made to do.
- It **cannot see any other builder's session**.

The control host above it does very little. It does not review work, does not score workers,
and will not be extended into doing so. Any judgement of quality has to come out of what you
design here, or it does not exist.

## What I am actually asking

**Design the artefact that tells a builder everything it needs to deliver one slice, and nothing
it does not.** I want the artefact itself, with named sections and named fields — not a
description of a philosophy. Where a field has a format, give the format. Where something is
pointed at rather than copied in, say what the pointer is and how the builder is expected to
follow it.

Then answer these, concretely, because they are where the design usually falls apart:

1. **Sizing.** What makes a slice correctly sized versus a planning failure disguised as a slice?
   Give a rule I could apply when writing one, not an intuition.

2. **Iteration cost.** In a Ralph loop the same context is re-read every wake. What is the
   dominant driver of token burn in the arrangement you propose, and what specifically did you do
   to make the *second* iteration cheap rather than merely the first?

3. **The stopping condition.** How does the builder know it is done, in a way that does not
   require a human and does not require the control host to grow a reviewer? Be concrete about
   what it checks and what it does when the check passes but the work is wrong.

4. **The brief is wrong.** The builder discovers that the slice as written does not match the
   code, or contradicts itself, or asks for something already decided against. What does it do,
   and what does that cost? Note that I do not want drift silently corrected and I do not want
   the loop dead-ended either.

5. **The specification is overruled.** These documents are authoritative, but a human above them
   can change their mind. That has to be representable. Where does the change live so that a
   builder does not read stale authority, and so that the document and the change cannot
   disagree?

6. **The escape hatch.** A builder that cannot deliver the slice at all. What does it do, and
   who finds out?

7. **What the builder hands back.** When the slice is done, what does it leave behind, in what
   form, and who reads it? Be careful here — I want the minimum that is still useful.

8. **What you would refuse.** Name anything in the space above you think is the wrong instinct,
   anything you think is commonly proposed that is actually harmful, and anything in my framing
   above you think I have got wrong. An answer that agrees with everything above without
   reservation has not engaged with the problem.

## Constraints

- The three documents stay canonical and stay where they are. You are not redesigning them.
- No new human in the loop per slice. I am asking what can be automated about this, not what a
  team should do manually.
- The builder cannot be trusted, and cannot be supervised continuously. Assume it is competent and
  fallible.
- Cost matters. The obvious answer to most of these questions is "give it less," and that is
  usually wrong — essential precision is cheaper than rework. Say where you are deliberately
  spending tokens up front and why.

Prefer one coherent design over a menu. If you think part of the setup is wrong, build the design
you think is right and tell me what you changed and why.
